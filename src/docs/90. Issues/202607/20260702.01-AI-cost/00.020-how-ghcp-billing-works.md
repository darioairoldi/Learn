## 🎯 Why Copilot is a distinct cost stream

GitHub Copilot is the behavioral side of AI consumption — it shows how developers actually use AI day to day. In the [AI cost platform](00.000-overview.md), it's <mark>Stream B</mark>: a usage stream whose cost is billed through GitHub, not through Azure Cost Management meters like Foundry.

That distinction is the whole point of this article. Copilot has its own licensing model, its own metering unit, and its own billing surface. To fold it into a unified dashboard, you need to understand all three, and know where GitHub spend can still land on your Azure invoice.

> Copilot billing moved to <mark>**GitHub AI Credits**</mark> on June 1, 2026. This article focuses on that current model. The older <mark>premium-request</mark> model — still relevant for some legacy annual plans and historical data — is documented in the separate [legacy premium-request appendix](00.022-ghcp-legacy-premium-request-billing.md).

## 🪪 Seat licensing and plans

The foundation of Copilot cost is <mark>per-seat licensing</mark>: each active user consumes one seat per billing cycle, regardless of how many machines or IDEs they use.

| Plan | Price | Audience |
|---|---|---|
| Copilot Free | Not applicable | Individuals trying Copilot (limited features, auto model selection only) |
| Copilot Student | Free (verified students) | Students |
| Copilot Pro | <mark>**$10 USD/month**</mark> (free for some) | Individual developers |
| Copilot Pro+ | <mark>**$39 USD/month**</mark> | Individual power users, premium models |
| Copilot Max | <mark>**$100 USD/month**</mark> | Highest individual monthly credit allowance |
| Copilot Business | <mark>**$19 USD/granted seat/month**</mark> | Organizations and teams |
| Copilot Enterprise | <mark>**$39 USD/granted seat/month**</mark> | Enterprises on GitHub Enterprise Cloud |

For a FinOps platform, the two seat-based plans — <mark>Business</mark> and <mark>Enterprise</mark> — are what matter most, because they carry both a per-seat license cost and a pooled usage allowance.

## 🪙 The GitHub AI Credits model

Under usage-based billing (current for Business and Enterprise), Copilot usage is measured in <mark>**GitHub AI Credits**</mark>.

### How credits are calculated

Every Copilot interaction consumes tokens — input, output, and cached. Each token is priced by the model used, and the total is converted into credits at a fixed rate:

$$
1 \text{ AI credit} = \$0.01 \text{ USD}
$$  

The cost of an interaction therefore depends on two things: <mark>the model</mark> and <mark>the number of tokens</mark>. A quick question on a lightweight model can cost a fraction of a credit; a long agent session on a frontier model across many files costs far more.

### Included credits and pooling

Each assigned license comes with a monthly allowance of <mark>included AI credits</mark>:

| Plan | Included AI credits per user per month | Promotional (Jun 1 – Sep 1, 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Copilot Business | <mark>1,900</mark> | 3,000 |
| Copilot Enterprise | <mark>3,900</mark> | 7,000 |

Key behaviors:

-   <mark>Pooled at the billing entity</mark>. An enterprise with 100 Business seats gets one shared pool of 190,000 credits, not 100 separate buckets. Power users draw more; lighter users offset them.
-   <mark>Adding seats mid-cycle</mark> increases the pool immediately; removing seats only shrinks it at the next cycle.
-   <mark>No rollover</mark>. Unused credits are forfeited, and the pool resets to the full amount at 00:00:00 UTC on the first of each month.

### What is and isn't billed in credits

-   <mark>Billed in credits</mark>: Copilot Chat, Copilot CLI, Copilot cloud agent, Copilot Spaces, Spark, and third-party coding agents.
-   <mark>Not billed (unlimited on paid plans)</mark>: code completions and next edit suggestions.

That last point is important: <mark>the everyday autocomplete experience doesn't draw down credits at all</mark>.  
Credit consumption is driven by chat, agents, and other higher-compute features.

## ✖️ Model multipliers and what consumes usage

In the AI Credits model, <mark>model choice</mark> is what makes one interaction cost more than another, because each model prices its tokens differently.

-   <mark>Lightweight models cost a fraction of a credit per interaction; frontier reasoning models cost far more.
-   <mark>Auto</mark> model selection routes to a suitable model at a discounted rate — the cheapest default for most prompts.
-   The legacy premium-request model expressed this same idea as a per-model <mark>multiplier</mark> (see the [legacy appendix](00.022-ghcp-legacy-premium-request-billing.md)).

For attribution, this means <mark>model choice is a first-class cost driver</mark> inside Copilot, exactly as it is for Foundry. Two developers sending the same number of prompts can generate very different costs based on the models they pick.

## 🎛️ Budgets, cost centers, and overage

When the pooled credits are exhausted, what happens next depends on policy:

-   <mark>Additional usage allowed</mark>: usage continues at published per-credit rates and is charged to the org or enterprise.
-   <mark>Additional usage not allowed</mark>: usage is blocked until the next cycle.

Budgets can be set at several levels, and they compose:

-   <mark>User-level budgets</mark> cap an individual's draw from both the pool and overage (a $0 budget blocks the user immediately).
-   <mark>Cost-center budgets</mark> cap metered charges for a defined group of users after the pool is exhausted.
-   <mark>Organization-level budgets</mark> cap charges for users whose seats bill to that organization.
-   <mark>Enterprise spending limits</mark> cap total metered charges across the enterprise.

Budgets are set in US dollars while usage displays in credits, drawing down at the fixed 1 credit = $0.01 rate (a $10 budget covers 1,000 credits). There's no automatic fallback to cheaper models when a budget is exhausted — usage simply stops.

> <mark>**Cost centers**</mark> are the direct bridge to the platform's chargeback dimension. Mapping GitHub cost centers to `dim_organization` lets Copilot spend flow into the same company/team allocation as cloud AI spend.

## 🔗 How Copilot spend reaches your Azure bill

Although GitHub manages Copilot billing, that spend can still land on your Azure invoice:

-   <mark>Org-to-subscription mapping</mark>: each GitHub organization is linked to a specific Azure subscription for billing. All that org's GitHub usage — Copilot included — flows to that subscription.
-   <mark>Pre-Purchase plans</mark>: GitHub Pre-Purchase plans are commit units (GitHub Commit Units, GCUs) bought at a discount. One GCU pays down one US dollar of qualifying GitHub retail cost. They cover Copilot, GitHub Enterprise, Advanced Security, Actions, Codespaces, and more.
-   <mark>Metered SKUs</mark>: Spark and cloud agent are tracked as dedicated SKUs for clearer cost visibility.

This mapping is why Copilot can appear both in GitHub's billing surface *and* in Azure Cost Management for the linked subscription — a reconciliation detail the platform must handle to avoid double counting.

## 🧱 What this means for attribution

Copilot is unusually rich for attribution because GitHub already knows the user, seat, and organization — the data cloud billing lacks.

| Copilot cost fact | Natural attribution key | Platform dimension |
|---|---|---|
| Seat license (per user) | Assigned user | <mark>`dim_user`</mark>, <mark>`dim_organization`</mark> |
| AI credits consumed | User + model + tokens | <mark>`dim_user`</mark>, <mark>`dim_model`</mark> |
| Cost-center budget | Cost center | <mark>`dim_organization`</mark> (cost center) |
| Repository / team activity | Repo, team | <mark>`dim_team`</mark>, <mark>`dim_application`</mark> |

Ingestion strategy for the platform:

1.  Land <mark>raw Copilot usage and seat metrics unchanged</mark> in Bronze (`bronze_github_copilot_usage_raw`).
2.  In Silver, map to <mark>canonical dimensions</mark> (<mark>user</mark>, <mark>team</mark>, <mark>org/cost center</mark>, <mark>model when present</mark>, <mark>repository</mark>).
3.  Keep the <mark>**license cost**</mark> and the <mark>**usage (credit) cost**</mark> as two components, then combine them in reporting rather than blending them into false precision.
4.  Join with cloud runtime cost to compute unit economics like accepted suggestions per active user and cost per active developer.

> For the concrete data sources, endpoints, and Bronze tables behind step 1, see [GitHub Copilot usage data sources for the Bronze layer](00.021-ghcp-usage-data-sources.md).

## ✅ Key takeaways

-   Copilot is billed through **GitHub**, on a **per-seat** license plus a **usage allowance** — a separate surface from Azure Cost Management.
-   The current usage unit is **AI Credits** (1 credit = $0.01), token-based and **pooled at the billing entity**, with **Business 1,900** and **Enterprise 3,900** included credits per user per month.
-   **Code completions and next edit suggestions are unlimited** and never billed in credits; chat, CLI, and agents are what consume them.
-   **Model choice**, **cost centers**, and **org-to-subscription mapping** are the three hooks that let Copilot spend be attributed and reconciled alongside cloud AI cost.
-   The **legacy premium-request** model (some annual plans, historical data) is documented in a separate [appendix](00.022-ghcp-legacy-premium-request-billing.md).
